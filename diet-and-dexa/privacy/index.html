Diet & DEXA: The Full Picture, by Useful Llama, is an app for tracking food, DEXA body-composition scans, exercise and (if you choose) fasting. Useful Llama is responsible for the information it receives to provide this app. Contact us about privacy or for help at useful.llama@gmail.com.

In short: your diary stays on your phone. Useful Llama has no server of its own and no account system. There are no ads, no analytics and no selling or sharing of your data. Some features send information to other services only when you use them, as explained below.

What stays on your phone

The app stores on your phone: your profile (sex, age, height, weight, goals, activity level, pregnancy or breastfeeding if you tell it), your food diary and meal photos, water, weights, DEXA and body-scan results, exercise, runs and their routes, fasting history, alarms and notification settings, chats with Dex, meal combos, meal-plan preferences, and app settings.

The app also learns on the phone: it keeps small "fingerprints" of the meals you save so it can recognise them next time (you can turn this off or make it forget in Settings), and it remembers the layout of body-scan reports it has read before. The food recogniser and the report reader run on the phone.

Settings has Export diary (JSON) (a file you save or share yourself) and Delete all my data, which removes the diary, photos, scans, runs, learned meals and learned report layouts from the phone. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data also removes them. Android may include app data in your device's own backup or transfer, depending on your settings; that is separate from the app's optional Google backup below.

Camera and photos

With your permission the camera is used to photograph meals, scan barcodes, photograph pages of body-scan reports, and for the live rep counter. Rep-counter frames are processed live and not recorded. Photos you pick from your gallery use Android's photo picker; the original stays where it was. Meal photos are kept on the phone with the meal.

Location, runs and maps

Location (precise or approximate) is used only while you use the app: during a GPS run you start (with a notification while it records) and for "Route ideas near you". Runs and routes stay on the phone and are not included in the Google backup.

  • The run map is Google Maps (Maps SDK for Android). Showing it sends map requests for the area to Google, along with technical information the SDK collects, under Google's privacy policy.
  • Route ideas: the suburb name is worked out by the phone's own location service (Android's geocoder, run by Google). With Claude connected, only that suburb name goes to Claude, which searches the web for real running spots, and the phone's geocoder then looks up each place's name to pin it on the map. Without Claude, a position rounded to about 100 metres is sent to the OpenStreetMap Overpass service to find nearby parks, tracks and paths.

Optional Claude (your own Anthropic account)

You can connect Claude, an AI by Anthropic, with your own Anthropic API key. The key is stored only on your phone and is never backed up. You choose the model and pay Anthropic directly for use. When you use a Claude feature, the app sends what that feature needs to Anthropic: meal photos (for photo reading and "Check with Claude"), body-scan PDFs or photos, exercise descriptions, your messages to Dex with the app numbers Dex uses to answer (such as your targets and today's totals), meal-plan preferences, and a suburb name for route ideas. Claude may run web searches for route ideas. Anthropic handles this under its own terms and privacy policy. Without a key, nothing is sent to Anthropic.

AI on the phone, speech and Health Connect

On supported phones the app uses Google's on-device ML Kit (object detection, picture labels, text recognition and barcodes) and Gemini Nano through Google Play services. This processing happens on the phone, but Google may receive technical information such as device and app details, diagnostics and identifiers, and models may be downloaded.

The microphone button in Dex uses Android's speech recogniser. Your phone's speech provider receives the audio and returns text to the app, under that provider's policy.

If you allow it, the app reads (never writes) your weight, steps and active calories from Health Connect to show progress and set targets. Weights it reads become part of your diary.

Food data and other services

Scanning a barcode sends only the barcode number to Open Food Facts to look up the product. Food values, world-dish recipes and fast-food menu figures are built into the app. Sharing (for example Share progress or a report) goes only to the app or person you choose in Android's share sheet. Notifications (eating-window notices and alarms, Dex check-ins, the run-in-progress notice) are created on the phone.

Optional backup with Google

If you choose Back up with Google, you sign in with your Google account and the app saves your diary as one document in Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore, Sydney, Australia), readable only by your account. It stores your Google account email and user ID with the backup. Meal photos, runs and routes, and your Claude key are not included. You can restore, stop backing up, or delete the backup in Settings; deleting it removes the document and its Firebase sign-in account.

Purchases

The one-time unlock is sold through Google Play. The app only learns whether the unlock is owned; Google Play handles payment under its own terms, and we never see your card details.

Private feedback

Private feedback ("How are we doing?") is optional and separate from a Google Play review. When you press Send, the app sends your rating from 1 to 5, any note you enter (up to 1,000 characters), and the app's name and version to Useful Llama's Google Form over an encrypted connection. The response is stored in Google Forms and its linked response spreadsheet for us to read and use to understand problems and improve the app. Google also records the submission time. A private monthly summary of responses may be emailed to useful.llama@gmail.com.

The app does not attach your name, email address, account, device identifier or diary to this request. Your note can identify you if you include personal or health details, so please leave them out. Google receives connection information when handling the request and processes it under its own privacy policy. Feedback is not published by Useful Llama.

If you have not bought the unlock, your first feedback that the form confirms adds 14 days to your free trial, whatever stars you choose; the app records on the phone that this thank-you was given. A Google Play rating or review never earns a reward. We keep feedback while it helps us improve the app; to find, correct or delete a response, email us with the app name, the approximate time you sent it and a short exact excerpt from your note. Deleting the app or its data does not delete feedback already sent.

Food recognition reports

When your phone names a food in a photo wrongly, you can tap Wrong food?, type what it really was or choose it from a list, and press Send. This is optional and happens only when you press Send, at most 5 times a day. The app then sends, over an encrypted connection to a Google Apps Script run by Useful Llama, which adds it to a private Google Sheet: what your phone guessed, what you typed, the app and recogniser versions, the app's language, and two lists of numbers your phone made from the photo's look (the same numbers the app's food recogniser reads on the phone).

Help the food camera learn. The same kind of report is also sent automatically in two cases: when you log a meal that Claude read (if you have connected Claude), and when you change, add or remove the foods your phone named in a photo before saving the meal. It contains what your phone's own recogniser guessed for that photo, the foods you logged, the versions and language, and the same two lists of numbers - so the free recogniser can learn what the food really was. Meals you save without changing the phone's foods send nothing. The first time this could happen, the app tells you it is on; you can turn it off at any time in Settings > Help the food camera learn, and nothing is sent before you have seen that notice. At most 20 such reports are sent a day.

The photo itself is never sent, and neither are your name, email address, account, location, device identifier or diary. The numbers cannot practically be turned back into the photo. Please don't type personal or health details in the answer. We use these reports only to train the app's food recogniser, keep them for up to 24 months, and do not publish or sell them. Google receives connection information when handling the request and processes it under its own privacy policy. Because a report carries nothing that identifies you, we usually cannot find a single report later; to ask anyway, email us the approximate time and what you typed.

Your choices and rights

You can export or delete your data in the app at any time. You can also write to useful.llama@gmail.com to ask about information Useful Llama holds about you; because there is no Useful Llama server, that is usually only email you send us, any private feedback and any food recognition reports. For data held by Google, Anthropic, Open Food Facts or OpenStreetMap, their own controls and policies apply. Depending on where you live you may have further privacy rights, including complaining to a privacy regulator. Nothing in this policy reduces rights the law gives you.
