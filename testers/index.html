Help test our apps

Before Google lets a new app onto the Play Store for everyone, real people have to test it for 14 days. Pick any apps you like the look of below. Each one shows what it is like, with real screenshots from the app.

How to join

  1. Join the tester group (once is enough for every app): open the group and press Join group. We only use it to give you access to the tests. (Cats vs Llamas has its own group, linked on its card.)
  2. On an app below, press Become a tester, then Become a tester again on Google's page.
  3. When it says You are a tester, tap download it on Google Play and install the app.

Android phones only. Use the same Google account as your phone's Play Store. Please keep each app installed for at least 14 days (that's the most important part), use it now and then, and tell me anything confusing, broken or annoying: useful.llamita@gmail.com.

Diet & DEXA

Health

Your food, your body scans and your exercise in one clear picture.

Point the camera at your plate for an estimate of calories, protein and key nutrients against Australian targets (estimates only, not medical advice). Add a DEXA or body-composition scan report and it explains each number in plain colours, for general information, not medical advice (privacy). Snap a treadmill or bike screen and it works out your workout's calories. Tell Dex, the fitness cuy (a guinea pig), what you ate or ask him anything.

Picture this

Mia has just had her first DEXA scan and has no idea what “visceral fat 105 cm²” means. She photographs the report and the app shows where each number sits for her age. At lunch she snaps her plate and sees she is short on fibre for the day.

What to try

  • Log a few meals, with the food camera at least once
  • Add a scan report if you have one
  • Photograph a gym machine's screen after a workout, or track a walk or a run

Everything is open free for your first 14 days, so you don't need to buy anything to test.

Drivista

Driving

Your phone is your dash cam.

Mount your phone, start recording while parked, and drive. Keep the clips that matter, and turn them into a tidy incident report with photos, a map and your own account, ready as a PDF.

Picture this

Sam starts recording before the morning commute. A car runs a red light just ahead. Once he is parked, he saves that clip so it is never recorded over, and builds a report for his insurer with the clip's photos and the map pin.

What to try

  • Record a drive (set it up while parked)
  • Save a clip and look through your footage
  • Start an incident report and export the PDF

Never touch the phone while driving. Testers get a free unlock code in the tester group.

Funspañol

Learn Spanish

Learn Spanish from the words you already know.

Lots of English words turn into Spanish with a simple pattern: information → información, university → universidad. Build on that with short lessons from beginner to advanced, chats with friendly neighbours, speaking practice and quick games.

Picture this

Priya is going to Mexico in two months. On the bus she does a ten-minute lesson, answers a neighbour's question out loud, and finishes with a lightning round of words she saved yesterday.

What to try

  • Do a few lessons, starting from Lo esencial
  • Try a speaking activity
  • Play one of the games

Crimson Defiance: Crater Road

Game

Tower defence on Mars: hold 50 waves.

The enemy is coming down the road and your HQ is at the end of it. Build towers beside the road, place soldiers and tanks, wall the road off, and stop every wave. Play either side, on four battlefields, with a boss every tenth wave.

Picture this

With ten minutes to spare, Jordan picks the Solar Legion, puts a laser tower on the first bend and a wall across the road, and sees how many waves they can hold before the titan arrives.

What to try

  • Play some waves on each side
  • Upgrade a tower and build a wall
  • Tell me which waves feel too easy or too hard

In-game coins are earned in each run. Watching an ad is always your choice, never forced.

Scribblito

Learn to write

Learn to write every letter and number with your finger.

A cheerful pencil shows each letter big across the screen, says how it sounds and guides your finger, in English or Spanish. Pick simple print, print with little tails or joined cursive. Every try gets an honest grade from D to A+, with confetti for good work and kind words when it isn't there yet.

Picture this

Noor is five and learning the alphabet at the kitchen table. Today's letter is B. The pencil shows the way, a small finger follows it, and up pops a gold badge and a picture of a bee.

What to try

  • Trace a few letters and numbers
  • Switch to Spanish or a harder handwriting level
  • Tell me if any grade feels unfair

No ads, no accounts, no purchases and no internet needed.
